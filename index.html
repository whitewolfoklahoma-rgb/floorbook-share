Pricing rules

Extras (transitions, baseboard, removal…) carry their own material and labor. Rather than track every stick of quarter-round, FloorBook costs extras at this % of what you charge for them. 55% is a sane planning default — calibrate it against a few finished jobs.

How FloorBook prices a job

The takeoff. Each room's net sq ft is length × width (or your measured sq ft for L-shaped rooms — measure the rectangles and add them). Order quantity = net × (1 + waste %). Waste is not padding; it's the cuts, the pattern matching, the damaged plank, and the closet corner. Under-order and the job stalls for a week waiting on a dye lot. Over-order and the profit sits on the customer's shelf.

Material is charged and costed on the order quantity (you buy the waste too). Labor is charged and costed on the net installed area (you don't install the waste). Carpet and sheet vinyl show the order quantity in sq yards, the way they're sold.

Three prices. Break-even is your true cost: material cost + crew cost + extras cost + consumables + travel + overhead. Recommended is the higher of your itemized rate-card total and the price that delivers your target margin — so a cheap material with expensive labor can't sneak through underpriced. Premium is recommended + 15%, for rush or difficult jobs.

The profit floor. Set a minimum margin once. If the final price you type falls below the price that delivers that margin, FloorBook warns you in red and won't save the quote unless you deliberately tick the override. Strategic discounts are your call; accidents are what it stops.

Calibrate your defaults. The waste table and rates are national planning numbers. Your supplier's prices and your crew's speed are the truth: after each job, compare what you ordered against what came back, and what the labor actually cost. Ten minutes in Settings after a few jobs makes every future quote sharper.

What FloorBook is not. It doesn't draw room diagrams or do laser-measure imports, it doesn't order material, and it doesn't take payments or send invoices — it's the estimator and the job book. You keep collecting money however you already do. Estimates are planning numbers, not guarantees: verify site conditions, subfloor, and moisture before you quote a final price.
